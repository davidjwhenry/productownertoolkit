# Research Report Output Format

Use this template for every desktop research report. Follow the structure exactly; adjust section depth based on evidence available.

---

## Template

```markdown
# Research Report: [Topic]

**Research goal:** [One sentence — why this research was conducted]
**Sources consulted:** [List of source types used, e.g. Reddit, G2, TechCrunch, competitor sites]
**Date:** [YYYY-MM-DD]

---

## TL;DR

[3–5 bullet points. Each bullet is a distinct, actionable finding — not a category header. Write these last, after the full report is complete.]

---

## Key themes

### [Theme 1 name]

[2–4 sentences describing the theme. What do users/reviewers/commenters say? How strong is the signal — single source or cross-source? Include a representative quote if one exists.]

> "[Direct quote]" — [Source, e.g. Reddit r/ProductManagement, G2 review, TechCrunch 2024]

### [Theme 2 name]

[Same structure]

### [Theme 3 name]

[Same structure]

[Add more themes as warranted. Aim for 3–6 themes total. Do not pad with weak themes — mark them as "weak signal" instead.]

---

## Competitive landscape

[Only include if competitor research was in scope. 2–4 sentences per competitor. Focus on: positioning, differentiators, pricing signals, user sentiment, notable gaps.]

| Competitor | Positioning | Strengths (from reviews/community) | Weaknesses (from reviews/community) |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Name] | [1 sentence] | [Key strengths] | [Key weaknesses] |

[If no competitor research was in scope, omit this section entirely.]

---

## User sentiment summary

[Only include if review sites or community sources were consulted. Summarise the overall sentiment pattern — not an average score, but what users are actually saying in aggregate. Note whether sentiment differs across segments, use cases, or company sizes.]

**Recurring praise:** [What do satisfied users consistently highlight?]
**Recurring complaints:** [What do dissatisfied users consistently raise?]
**Neutral observations:** [What do users note without strong sentiment — often important for product decisions?]
```

## Section guidance

### TL;DR
Write this last. Each bullet should stand alone as a finding a stakeholder could act on. Avoid category headers ("Users have mixed feelings") — write the finding ("Users consistently praise onboarding speed but churn at the analytics tier due to pricing complexity").

### Key themes
Themes should reflect what the research actually found, not pre-conceived categories. Name themes after the insight, not the topic (e.g. "Pricing opacity drives switching intent" not "Pricing").

Cross-source themes — where the same pattern appears on Reddit, G2, and in press coverage — carry more weight than single-source observations. Note this explicitly.

### Weak signal
If a theme is supported by only one or two sources, or the sources are low-quality, flag it: "**Weak signal** — only one source; treat as directional."

### Evidence gaps
Always include this section. A research report without stated gaps creates false confidence. Be specific about what was searched and not found.
